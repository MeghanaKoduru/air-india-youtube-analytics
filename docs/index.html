Key takeaways

  1. Attention concentrated in a few outlets. The top 5 of 42 channels captured 52.2% of the 45.0M total views; the top 5 individual videos alone took 24.5%. India Today led on volume — 21 videos (22.6% of all coverage) for 10.8M views — while CNN and CNN-News18 earned the highest per-video averages (~0.8–1.0M).
  2. Human-story framing won the view counts. 19 of 93 titles (20.4%) mention the survivor; 4 of the top 10 videos by views center the survivor or victims. Short verified-footage clips converted best — NDTV’s 1.1-minute CCTV clip earned ~1.55M views per minute of runtime, the dataset’s highest.
  3. Comment tone is sombre, not hostile. 45.3% negative · 29.2% positive · 25.5% neutral (mean compound −0.10) — but the top bigrams are rest peace, condolences families, loved ones: grief language that VADER scores negative by construction.
  4. A large speculation thread runs alongside the mourning. boeing is the #3 comment keyword (1,465 mentions); landing gear, black box, and pilot error all rank in the top-10 bigrams. For moderation teams, the actionable surface is unverified speculation — not “negativity,” which is mostly condolence.
  5. Compassion resonates most per comment. Positive comments average 11.0 likes vs 9.0 (negative) and 5.4 (neutral) — hope, solidarity, and the survivor’s story are the most audience-validated messages.
  6. Tone was stable across all five scraped days. The negative share stayed within 42.8–48.7% with no escalation — volume decayed after the ~day-3 peak (7,860 → 1,585 comments) but the mix barely moved.

Limitations & ethics

  • Single-video comment sample: sentiment describes one Firstpost thread, not all crash coverage.
  • Missing video metrics: the source data has no video likes, comment counts, publish dates, or subscriber counts — engagement-rate and upload-time analyses were skipped, never imputed.
  • Approximate dates: relative labels resolved against the scrape date (±1 day).
  • VADER is English-tuned: Hinglish and Devanagari comments skew neutral; sarcasm and mourning idiom are imperfectly captured.
  • This dashboard describes media coverage and public discourse around a tragedy in which 260+ people died. It assigns no blame and draws no conclusions about the crash’s cause.
